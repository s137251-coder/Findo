### Full description

```
Findo is hidden somewhere in this crowd. The clock is running.

A hundred levels, each one big hand-drawn scene packed with hundreds of people going about their day: a town square around a fountain, a working farm, a night market, an aquarium, a theatre at the interval, a fish market at dawn, a night festival lit by lanterns. Somewhere in every one of them, a girl with brown braids, a yellow top and a purple skirt is standing perfectly still, waiting to be spotted.

HOW IT PLAYS
• Drag to move around the scene, pinch to zoom right in
• Study the crowd. She looks like everyone else until she doesn't
• Tap her and the level is yours
• Tap the wrong person and it costs you points and precious seconds
• The faster you find her, the bigger the time bonus
• Every level is rated one to three stars, so there is always a better run to chase
• Play a level again and she is hiding somewhere else, so chasing a better time is a real search and not a memory test

IT GETS HARDER, AND NOT BY SHRINKING HER
The crowds grow from a couple of hundred people to nine hundred. Scenes turn yellow, then purple, then dark, so a yellow top stops being the thing that catches your eye. And the crowd fills with near-misses: brown braids on the wrong outfit, the right top on the wrong person, two of her three signatures on someone who is not her. By level ninety the clock is under a minute.

THE DAILY HUNT
One level a day, the same one for every player in the world, at the highest difficulty the game has. No hints. One attempt. Your time goes on a leaderboard that everyone shares, and a new hunt opens every day.

CAN'T SEE HER?
A hint sweeps the map towards Findo and makes her glow. Start with three, earn more by watching a video, or buy a pack.

NEW TO IT? THE FIRST LEVEL TEACHES YOU
A guided first hunt has you drag, pinch and use a hint on the real map, one step at a time, and short tips explain the rest as you reach it.

PLAYS YOUR WAY
• Full English and Hebrew, with proper right-to-left layout
• Switch language any time, mid-level, without restarting
• No account needed. Sign in with Google only if you want your levels, stars, hints and purchases on every phone
• An optional evening reminder when a new Daily Hunt is waiting
• Share your best times with friends
• Works on phones and tablets, in portrait and landscape

Findo is free to play. It contains ads and optional in-app purchases, including a one-time purchase that removes ads permanently.
```

**What the leaderboard stores, and why it is declared.** Playing the daily hunt
writes three things to the game's server: an anonymous identifier Firebase gives
the install, the time taken, and a display name the game generates ("Curious
Tiger 147"). The player never types a name and never signs in, so no personal
information is involved — but the identifier and the time leave the device, and
anything that leaves the device is collection.

**Account deletion (required because the game now offers sign-in).** In-app:
*Settings → Account → Delete account* deletes the save, the player's week and
all-time row, and the Firebase account. Outside the app, for the Play Console
"Delete account URL" field, use the privacy policy's deletion section:
`https://s137251-coder.github.io/Findo/privacy-policy.html` — it explains the
in-app path and the e-mail route.

**Deletion.** Uninstalling removes everything held on the device. The
leaderboard row is tied to an anonymous identifier with no way to reach the
person behind it, and a player can ask for it to be deleted through the contact
address in the privacy policy.

Game progress, language and audio settings live in `shared_preferences` on the
device. On-device storage is **not** "collection" under Play's definition, so it
is not declared.

| --- | --- |
| Category | Game |
| Violence, blood, or realistic violence | No |
| Sexual content or nudity | No |

| Drugs, alcohol, tobacco | No |
| Gambling, or simulated gambling | No — see below |
| User-generated content or user interaction | No — see below |
| Shares user location with other users | No |
| Digital purchases | **Yes** |
| Contains ads | **Yes** |

Expected outcome: **Everyone / PEGI 3** in most territories.

**Gambling.** Level 52 is a casino floor. A scene that *depicts* roulette tables
is not simulated gambling — the player never places a bet, they look for a girl
— so the answer stays No.

**User interaction.** The leaderboard shows other players' names and times.
Those names are generated by the game from a fixed word list, never typed, and
there is no message, no profile and no way for one player to reach another. That
is a scoreboard, not user-generated content, and not interaction. It is worth
knowing why the answer is No rather than answering by reflex: if a player were
ever allowed to type their own name, this answer changes, and so does the age
rating.

## Ads and target audience

- **Ads declaration:** yes, the app contains ads. Getting this wrong is a common
  rejection cause.
- **Target audience:** declare **13 and over**. Declaring an audience that
  includes under-13s triggers Google Play Families Policy, and the AdMob
  configuration in this app is not set up for child-directed treatment.
- **"Suitable for all ages" is not the same answer.** The privacy policy says
  the content suits every age (no violence, no mature content, no chat) and
  that the game is made for a general audience, not directed at under-13s. That
  matches an IARC rating of Everyone / 3+ together with a 13+ target audience.
  If the target audience is ever widened to include children, the policy, the ad
  configuration and this answer all have to change together.
