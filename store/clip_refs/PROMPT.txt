A vertical 9:16 video: a real girl walks into a drawn world, becomes a drawing
herself, and is lost in the crowd. Silent, no text anywhere in the frame.

THE GIRL, FROM THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
Brown hair in two long braids, a bright yellow sleeveless top with a black
V-shaped neckline, a mid-violet knee-length skirt, black shoes.

0.0 to 2.0 SECONDS - THE REAL GIRL
She stands on a plain white background, framed from the knees up, evenly lit.
She looks into the camera, smiles, and gives one small friendly wave. Camera
still.

2.0 to 3.5 SECONDS - A DRAWN WORLD BUILDS AROUND HER
The white background is replaced by the illustrated funfair from the THIRD
reference image: stalls, bunting, a carousel, a big wheel, flat vector
illustration with uniform thick black outlines, seen from a high
three-quarter aerial angle about 55 degrees looking down. Cartoon people walk
about. She stays a real, filmed girl with real skin, hair and fabric, standing
inside a drawing -- deliberately the only real thing in the frame. She turns
and walks away from the camera into the fair.

3.5 to 7.0 SECONDS - SHE WALKS IN AND THE CAMERA LEAVES HER BEHIND
She walks deeper into the fair. The camera rises and pulls back steadily and
does not stop. The fair opens out below: more stalls, more rides, more cartoon
people, until hundreds of them fill the frame. She keeps walking and keeps
getting smaller, because the camera keeps getting further away. By seven
seconds she is a tiny figure among hundreds of others, no taller than the
cartoon people walking beside her.

7.0 to 8.0 SECONDS - SHE BECOMES A DRAWING, ALREADY TINY
While she is already that small and already far away, she becomes a drawing.
The change sweeps up her once in under a second and she turns into the flat
cartoon girl in the SECOND reference image: uniform black outline, solid flat
fills, simple face. She turns to face the camera and stands still.

THE CAMERA DOES NOT MOVE CLOSER FOR THIS. It does not zoom in on her, it does
not cut to her, it does not follow her. It stays exactly where it was, looking
down at the whole fair. The change happens to a small figure in a wide picture.

HOW BIG SHE IS AT THE END - THIS IS WHAT THE LAST VERSION GOT WRONG
She is one thirtieth of the frame height. To be concrete:

- She is the same height as the cartoon children walking near her, and shorter
  than the cartoon adults.
- She is shorter than one of the small stall awnings behind her.
- Her face must NOT be readable. At this size a face is a few pixels: two dots
  and nothing more. If you can see her expression, she is far too big.
- She takes up a small part of the frame. Hundreds of other people are visible
  around her in every direction.

If she is the largest person in the frame, the shot is wrong. If she is in the
middle of the frame, the shot is wrong. She stands about a third of the way in
from one edge, with other people standing close on every side -- no clearing,
no gap, no empty ground around her.

8.0 SECONDS TO THE END
The camera holds completely still on the whole fair. Nothing moves except the
crowd going about its day. She stands among them, facing the camera, doing
nothing. A viewer should have to search for several seconds to find her.

THE OTHER RULE
Nobody else may wear all three of her signatures together. Other people may
have brown braids, or a yellow top, or a violet skirt, but never two on one
person and never all three. Plenty of the crowd wear yellow of other shades and
purple of other shades, so those colours are ordinary here.

Nothing points at her, circles her, lights her or marks her. No other character
looks at the camera.

DO NOT INCLUDE
No text, letters, numbers, signs, logos or watermarks. No arrows, circles,
sparkles, magic dust or highlights. No zoom in on the girl at any point. No
camera shake, no speed ramps. No music or speech.
